Plumbing Installation in Neptune, NJ

Plumbing installation covers putting new fixtures and piping into a home: sinks, toilets, tubs, showers, a kitchen with a disposal and dishwasher, or an entire bathroom added where none existed. The work splits into rough-in, which is the piping inside walls and floors, and finish, which is setting the fixtures and testing them. Our plumbers do both in Neptune.

Good installation depends on decisions made before anyone opens a wall: where the drain slopes, which pipe material fits, and which inspections apply. This page walks through those choices.

Fixtures we set, and what each one needs

Each fixture has its own connection points and clearances.

Kitchen and bath sinks

Faucet, supply lines, shutoff valves and a P-trap connected to the wall drain.

Toilets

A flange at the right height, a wax ring, closet bolts and a supply line with its own shutoff.

Tubs and showers

A valve in the wall, a drain with a trap, and a spout or showerhead on the right rough-in height.

Laundry and utility sinks

Standpipe or sink drain, hot and cold supply, and often a washer box.

Outdoor hose bibs

A frost-resistant spigot with a vacuum breaker, set with a slight pitch to drain.

Setting a toilet: flange, wax ring and closet bolts

Toilet installation is a good example of why small details matter. We cover removing a failed toilet in more depth on our toilet repair page.

  1. Check the flange

    It should sit level and firm on the finished floor. A cracked or low flange needs a repair ring before anything else.

  2. Place closet bolts

    The bolts slot into the flange and hold the bowl in position.

  3. Set the wax ring and bowl

    The bowl goes straight down onto the ring without twisting, then shims fill any gap on an uneven floor.

  4. Tighten and caulk

    Nuts go down in small turns to avoid cracking the porcelain, then the base is sealed as the local inspector or manufacturer prefers.

  5. Connect and test

    We attach the tank, the supply line and the shutoff, then flush repeatedly and watch the base for seepage.

PEX, copper, CPVC, PVC and cast iron: honest trade-offs

Each material has a place. No single one wins everywhere.

MaterialBest forTrade-off
PEXSupply lines in walls and floorsNot for sun exposure, needs correct fittings.
CopperSupply lines where durability mattersHigher material cost, skilled soldering.
CPVCHot and cold supply in some codesBrittle with age, sensitive to heat.
PVCDrain, waste and ventNot for hot water or supply use.
Cast ironQuiet drain stacks, older homesHeavy, costly, corrodes over decades.

The code decides what is allowed in a given location. New Jersey's plumbing subcode is the National Standard Plumbing Code, and we pick materials that comply.

Double kitchen sink, disposal and dishwasher

A double sink with a disposal shares one drain arrangement. Both bowls feed a common trap, the disposal attaches to one drain outlet, and the dishwasher hose connects to the disposal inlet or an air-gap fitting. The knockout plug inside a new disposal must be removed before the dishwasher hose is connected, or the dishwasher will not drain.

If you are adding a disposal to an existing sink, we check the trap layout and sink drain size first. For disposal problems after installation, see garbage disposal repair.

Adding a bathroom: rough-in and finish stages

A new bathroom needs a drain that slopes toward the stack, a vent so traps hold water, and hot and cold supply lines. Placing the new bathroom near an existing stack saves a lot of piping, and we look at the layout with you before you buy fixtures.

Rough-in happens before walls close. Finish happens after drywall and tile. Plan fixture choices before rough-in, since the rough-in heights depend on the specific model you buy.

Permits, fees and inspection stages

Permit rules vary, so ask the Neptune Township construction office what your project needs. The township's 2018 fee schedule lists $27 per plumbing fixture or device and a $95 minimum per subcode. Confirm current fees with the office, because the schedule may have changed.

For one and two family dwellings, the township lists rough plumbing, finish plumbing and final plumbing inspections. Rough plumbing is done before piping is covered. Inspections must be requested at least 24 hours ahead, and the township performs them within three business days of the requested time. In Belmar, the Construction Department can fine unpermitted work $500 to $2,000, so ask first.

Water quality, flood zones and floodplain permits

New Jersey American Water reports naturally occurring hardness of 72 to 140 ppm in its Coastal North system. Hard water leaves scale on faucet aerators and showerheads, so choose fixtures with removable aerators and accessible cartridges.

Large parts of Neptune's residential areas, including all of North Island, are in mapped flood zones. The township requires a Floodplain Development Permit before construction or development begins. If your project is in a flood zone, ask the office about that permit before ordering anything.

Every fixture deserves its own shutoff valve

A shutoff valve under each sink and behind each toilet lets you stop a leak in seconds without closing the whole house. We install quarter-turn valves and braided or solid supply lines at every fixture we set. Check the ones you already have, since old valves seize if they are never used.

Get the room ready

A little preparation shortens the visit.

  • Choose your fixtures before rough-in, and keep the spec sheets for us.
  • Clear cabinets and the floor around each fixture.
  • Tell us where the main shutoff is, or point us to it.
  • Decide whether you need a permit conversation with the township first.
  • For a bathroom addition, note the nearest stack and any wall you cannot open.
  • If you rent, get written approval from the landlord before changes.
