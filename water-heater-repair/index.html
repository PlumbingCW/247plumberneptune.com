A shower that turns cold after two minutes, a tank that rumbles like popping corn, or a puddle around the base all point to a failing water heater. Water heater repair means finding the part that quit, whether a thermostat, element, gas control, valve or the tank itself, and fixing only that. Our plumbers work on gas and electric tanks in Neptune, and the 24/7 emergency line is open around the clock.

Is the trouble heat, water, or both? A heater that makes no heat has a different cause than one that leaks. We check power or gas supply, controls, safety devices and the tank shell before recommending anything. If the tank has rusted through, we say so plainly and walk you through replacement options, because repairing a failed tank wastes your money.

No hot water or only lukewarm?

Lukewarm water usually means half a heater is working. On an electric unit, one of two elements has often burned out, so the top of the tank heats but the bottom does not, or the reverse. On a gas unit, a dirty burner, a weak thermocouple or a tripped flame sensor can keep the flame short or shut it off mid-cycle.

The technical name for what we do next is thermostat, element and gas-control diagnosis. We measure element resistance with a multimeter, check the high-limit reset, and test the gas valve and flame signal on gas models. That sequence tells us in minutes whether you need a cheap part or a new tank.

Water on the floor or a gas smell near the heater?

If you smell gas, leave the building now and call the gas utility or 911 from outside before you call any plumber. Do not flip switches, light anything or return for belongings. We can only work on the heater after the utility says the building is safe.

For a water leak with no gas smell, shut the cold supply valve on the pipe above the heater and turn off its power at the breaker, but only if the panel is dry and you are not standing in water.

If the floor is wet around electrical outlets or the panel, stay away and call us. A tank that pours water from the shell is finished, and a tank that drips from a fitting often can be saved.

What usually fails inside a tank heater

Most failures come down to a short list of parts. Knowing the list helps you judge whether a quote makes sense.

Thermostat or high-limit switch

A drifting thermostat gives lukewarm water. A tripped high-limit switch gives none until it is reset and the cause is found.

Heating element

Electric heaters carry two elements. Sediment cooks the lower one first, and it can burn out in a single season.

Pilot, igniter or thermocouple

On gas heaters, these parts keep the flame lit. A weak thermocouple lets the pilot drop out for no obvious reason.

Anode rod

This sacrificial rod corrodes so the tank steel does not. Once it is used up, the tank wall starts to rust.

Temperature and pressure relief valve

The T&P valve is a safety device. If it drips or discharges, the cause is excess pressure or heat, not a bad valve alone.

Sediment and Neptune's water supply

New Jersey American Water reports naturally occurring hardness of 72 to 140 ppm in its Coastal North system, which covers Neptune Township and Ocean Grove. That works out to roughly 4 to 8 grains per gallon, and your street may sit anywhere in that moderately hard to hard range. Minerals settle on the tank floor, insulate the burner or lower element, and cause the rumbling you hear.

The practical payoff is simple. Draining a few gallons each year, or flushing the tank fully, slows the buildup and keeps the lower element or burner working. This matters in places like Ocean Township and Neptune alike, since both draw from the same Coastal North system. If a heater has gone years without a flush, we may stop short of a hard flush, because loosening old scale can start a leak at a worn drain valve.

Elements, thermostats and the drain-first rule

Electric elements can be swapped, but the job is not a quick plug-and-go. Power goes off at the breaker, the supply closes, and the tank is drained below the element before the old one comes out. Anyone who tells you to skip draining is asking for a flooded floor or a shock.

Thermostats sit behind access panels and insulation. We test them before replacing, since a thermostat set wrong or a reset tripped by a failed element can look like a dead heater. Where the tank is old and corroded inside, a new element in a failing shell is money wasted, and we will say that before you pay for the part.

While you wait for us

These steps protect your home and speed up the diagnosis.

  • Close the cold supply valve above the heater if water is escaping.
  • Switch off the heater at its breaker or set a gas heater to pilot only if the area is dry and no gas odor is present.
  • Leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 if you smell gas.
  • Move boxes and stored items away from the base so we can reach the drain and fittings.
  • Photograph the data plate, the leak and any damage for your insurer or landlord.
  • Do not touch the T&P valve discharge pipe or hot fittings with bare hands.
