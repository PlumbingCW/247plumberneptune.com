Sprinkler System Leak Detection in Neptune, NJ

Find the leaking zone with a meter test and a walk of the lawn before anyone digs.

By the Neptune 24/7 Plumbing team · Published October 15, 2026 · Updated October 15, 2026

24/7 Emergency Line Open(732) 466-1098Call now!

The fastest way to find a sprinkler leak is to turn the system off, shut every fixture in the house, and watch your water meter. If the dial still moves, water is escaping somewhere. Then you run one zone at a time and note which one has soggy ground, low spray or a pressure drop.

Most irrigation leaks are cheap to fix and hide in plain sight, such as a cracked head, a split fitting or a stuck valve. Some are on the main line feeding the system, which is more serious. This guide shows the meter test, the zone walk, the role of the backflow preventer, and the point where you should hand it off.

The meter test in five steps

Do this on a quiet morning when nobody is using water.

  1. Turn off everything that uses water

    Shut taps, appliances and the irrigation timer. Check that the ice maker is not filling.

  2. Read the meter

    Open the meter cover. Note the sweep hand or the small triangle or dial that spins at low flow.

  3. Wait fifteen minutes

    Do not use any water. Come back and read it again.

  4. Compare the readings

    If the low-flow dial moved or the digits advanced, something is leaking on the property.

  5. Close the irrigation shutoff and repeat

    If the dial stops, the leak is in the sprinkler system. If it keeps moving, the leak is elsewhere in the house or service line.

Walking your zones one at a time

With the meter test done, run each zone and look for clues.

  • Run one zone for a few minutes and watch the spray pattern.
  • Look for geysers, mist or heads that sit crooked.
  • Walk the zone line and feel for spongy or sunken ground.
  • Listen at the valve box for hissing when zones are off.
  • Open the valve box and check for water pooling or wet wiring connectors.
  • Mark wet spots with a flag and photograph them.
  • Run the zone a second time to see whether the wet area grows.

What a professional adds

Some leaks do not show on the surface. A licensed plumber can isolate each zone with a pressure gauge, pressurize the line with a test plug and see which section loses pressure. An acoustic listening device then pinpoints the sound of escaping water through the soil, so the dig is as small as possible.

Thermal imaging and tracer gas are used in other leak jobs, but for irrigation the meter test, zone pressure test and listening device do most of the work. Ask the plumber to mark the spot and explain what they heard before any digging starts.

The backflow preventer on your sprinkler line

An irrigation system connects to drinking water, so a backflow preventer keeps lawn water from flowing backward into the house supply. Spray can leak from the vacuum breaker cap, which sits at the top of the device. A leaking breaker spits water when the system runs, and that is easy to mistake for a pipe break.

These devices are often required to be tested on a schedule. Rules vary by town, so ask the local office. A leaking or failed device should be repaired or replaced by a licensed plumber, since it protects the drinking water supply.

When the leak is not in the sprinkler

If the meter keeps moving with the irrigation shutoff closed, the leak is in the house or the pipe from the street to the house. A buried service line leak can run unnoticed for months. That is a job for leak detection with tools such as an acoustic listening device and a pressure test. The repair may fall under water service line repair.

Seasonal habits that prevent leaks

A few habits cut down on surprises.

Spring startup

Open valves slowly and walk each zone. Water hammer can crack fittings when the system is opened quickly.

Fall blowout

Drain or blow out the lines before the first freeze. Water left in pipes expands and splits them.

Mow with care

Heads struck by mowers crack. Flag them and keep them flush with the soil.

Check pressure

High pressure shreds nozzles and stresses fittings. A pressure reducing valve or regulator may help.
