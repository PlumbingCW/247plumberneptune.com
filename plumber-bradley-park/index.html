Bradley Park grew during the late 19th-century building boom that also built up Ocean Grove and Asbury Park. By 1900 it was one of the most densely populated parts of Neptune Township, and it is named for James A. Bradley, the founder of Asbury Park. Today it is mainly residential, with neat blocks of houses and State Highway 35 forming a business corridor through the middle.

Those facts point to the plumbing questions that come up most: drainage on ground that began as meadow, and sewer laterals running under mature street trees. This page explains how to sort out each one.

Built on filled meadowland: why basement water is worth checking

According to the township, the Bradley Park land was originally meadowlands that developers Frank Appleby and H.C. Winsor filled in. We cannot say how that affects any single house, because fill depth and drainage vary by lot. What it does mean is that a damp basement here deserves a proper look at where the water comes from.

Start by noting whether water appears after rain, after snowmelt or all the time. Groundwater seeping up through a floor often points to a sump pump, while water entering at a wall points to drainage outside. Our basement waterproofing page explains how the two differ.

Slow drains under tree-lined streets

The township describes wide, tree-lined streets, and tree roots are a common cause of sewer lateral trouble in any town. A root enters at a joint and catches paper and grease until the line clogs.

Gurgling in several drains

Air pushed back through traps suggests a partial blockage in the main line, not a single fixture.

Backup in the lowest drain

A floor drain or tub is the first place water appears when the lateral is blocked.

Repeat clogs after clearing

If the same drain slows every few months, ask for a camera look. The picture shows roots, a bellied pipe or scale.

Wet patch in the yard

A soft spot or lush strip over the line can mark a leak. Call before digging.

What a camera inspection tells you

A camera run through the cleanout shows the pipe material, the joints and any breaks. It is the difference between guessing at the cause and seeing it. After the inspection, drain cleaning or hydro jetting clears a blockage, and a damaged section needs a repair rather than another clearing.

A school site becoming a park

The Bradley Park School, built in 1897, is no longer in use, and the township says the site will become a neighborhood park funded by Green Acres. No completion date is given. If you live nearby, ask the township about construction timing and any street work, since digging near your lines can expose them. Mark your sewer and water lines before any nearby excavation.

Questions to ask before you hire for a sewer job

Use these with any plumber, including us.

  • Will you run a camera before and after the work.
  • Can I see the footage.
  • What is the license number, so I can verify it on the state lookup.
  • Is the quote for clearing, lining or replacing the pipe.
  • Does the work need a permit or inspection.
  • What does the price include if you find a second problem.
