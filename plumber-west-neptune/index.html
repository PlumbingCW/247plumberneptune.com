West Neptune is the oldest of Neptune Township's neighborhoods, and over the years it has been called Shark River Village, Trap, Greenville and Hamilton. Settlers arrived before the Revolutionary War for the waterways and rolling farmland, and the area still mixes very old houses with new construction.

That mix shapes plumbing decisions. A house from the 1800s and a recent subdivision home call for different questions, and this page explains how to find out what you actually have instead of guessing from the age of the building.

Old house, new house: find out what pipes you have

The township page says numerous homes from the late 1700s to the 1870s line Jumping Brook Road, Gully Road and Wayside Road, while Hamilton Gardens, Hamilton Park and Neptune Manor hold newer homes. Age alone does not tell you the pipe material, because plumbing is often replaced piece by piece. A visual check of the supply lines at the main and under sinks, plus a look at the water meter, gives better evidence.

If you plan a renovation or see rust-colored water, ask for a pipe material check before choosing a fix. Our repiping page explains when replacing supply lines makes sense and when a single repair is enough.

New construction and permits

The township describes new housing being built throughout West Neptune, and Route 66 development has brought a shopping complex anchored by a Home Depot at Jumping Brook Road. New work means inspections. The township's required plumbing inspections for one and two family homes include rough plumbing before walls close, finish plumbing and a final inspection.

Inspections must be requested at least 24 hours ahead, and the township says it performs them within three business days of the requested time. Permit rules vary, so ask the Neptune Township construction office. The township's 2018 fee schedule lists $27 per plumbing fixture or device and a $95 minimum per subcode, so confirm current fees with the office.

Water service lines and the lead replacement law

New Jersey law, P.L.2021, c.183, requires community water systems to replace all lead service lines within 10 years, the whole line from main to building. If your service line is old, ask your water utility whether it has been inspected. The customer service number on your water bill is the place to ask. A water service line repair is a separate job from a utility replacement, so ask before you dig.

Before you call about a leak or low pressure

Answers to these shorten the first call.

  • Where the main shutoff is and whether it turns.
  • Which fixtures are affected, one or all.
  • Whether the meter dial moves with every tap closed.
  • When the symptom started and whether anything changed first.
  • Whether the house has been renovated and when.
  • Photos of the pipe material under the sink or at the main.
