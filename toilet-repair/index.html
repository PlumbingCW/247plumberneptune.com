Toilet repair means finding which part has failed and fixing it, whether that is a flapper that leaks into the bowl, a fill valve that never shuts off, a wax ring that has lost its seal or a clog in the trap. If your toilet is overflowing or leaking onto the floor in Neptune, our 24/7 emergency line, (732) 466-1098, is open around the clock.

Close the shutoff valve behind the toilet first. Then call us, and we will tell you what to do while you wait.

Toilet running, clogged or leaking at the base?

A toilet that runs after the flush is not sealing in the tank. One that will not clear has a blockage in the trap just below the bowl. One that leaks around the bottom has lost the wax seal to the drain or has a loose connection.

Plumbers call the three problems a leaking flush valve, a closet-bend clog and a failed wax gasket. Each has a different fix, so naming the symptom correctly saves the wrong parts and a second visit.

Toilet overflowing onto the floor?

Do not flush again. Reach behind the toilet and turn the angle stop clockwise to shut off the water. If it will not turn, remove the tank lid and lift the float or close the flapper to stop the fill, then call (732) 466-1098.

Overflow water from a clogged toilet carries waste, so keep children and pets out and wear gloves when cleaning. Stay away from wet outlets and do not touch the electrical panel if water has reached it. Photograph the damage for your insurer or landlord. A toilet that is merely running with no spill is not an emergency, and you can book a same-day appointment when available.

Three quick tests for a running toilet

These checks take a few minutes and show the culprit.

  • Drop a few drops of food coloring in the tank. If color reaches the bowl without a flush, the flapper leaks.
  • Listen for a hiss after the tank is full. That points to the fill valve.
  • Check that the chain has a little slack and is not caught under the flapper.
  • Look for water trickling down the overflow tube, which means the fill level is too high.
  • Wipe the flapper edge. If the rubber is soft or slimy, replace it.
  • Shut the supply valve if you cannot find the source, and call us.

Rocking and base leaks: the wax ring story

A wax ring sits between the toilet and the drain flange, sealing sewer gas and water. When the toilet rocks, the seal breaks. The leak may be slow and stay hidden in the subfloor for months before a stain shows.

Fixing it means lifting the toilet, replacing the ring, checking that the flange sits at the right height and tightening the closet bolts evenly. A low flange needs a spacer or extension, and a rotted subfloor needs repair first. If you see a soft floor or a stained ceiling below, call us before using the toilet again.

Clogs: plunger first, auger second

A clog in the bowl usually clears with the right tool and a steady hand.

  1. Use a flange plunger

    Seat it fully over the drain and push gently, then pull sharply.

  2. Try a closet auger

    The curved tip reaches the trap without scratching the porcelain.

  3. Stop at repeats

    If it clogs twice in a day, the problem is past the bowl, and drain cleaning is the next step.

  4. Call for the line

    A second fixture draining slowly means the main line needs attention.

Hard water, old valves and Neptune bathrooms

Water here ranges from 72 to 140 ppm hardness, so your street may sit anywhere in that moderately hard to hard range. Minerals build on the flush valve seat and fill valve, which makes flappers stick open and fill valves hiss. That is why a flapper replaced years ago may need replacing again.

Older Neptune homes, including Ocean Grove's Victorian houses, may still have original shutoff valves that seize. Do not force a stuck angle stop, because the stem can snap and flood the room. Close the main water valve for the house instead and call us. Neighbors in Asbury Park with older bathrooms face the same issue. Shore towns such as Belmar have many seasonal homes, where a valve left untouched for months is more likely to stick.

When to replace instead of repair

A cracked bowl or tank, a toilet that repeatedly fails or one that wastes water may be better replaced than repaired. New toilets are set on a fresh wax ring with correct flange height, and we handle that job. For a bathroom remodel with several fixtures, see plumbing installation.
