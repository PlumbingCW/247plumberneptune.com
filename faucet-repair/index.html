A drip that keeps you awake, a spray from the base of the handle, or a stream that will not shut off all call for faucet repair. Faucet repair means identifying the faucet design, replacing the worn internal part, and checking the shutoff valves and supply lines that feed it. Our licensed plumbers repair kitchen, bathroom, tub, shower and outdoor faucets across Neptune, and the 24/7 emergency line is open around the clock.

Many faucets are worth saving, since the body is solid brass and only a cartridge or seal has worn. Some are not, and we say which is which before you pay for parts.

Faucet dripping, spraying or no pressure?

A steady drip usually means a worn seat, seal or cartridge that no longer shuts the water off. Spray from the handle or base points to a failed O-ring or packing. Weak flow at one faucet is often a clogged aerator, while weak flow at every faucet is a different problem entirely.

The technical name for most of these repairs is cartridge, seat and seal replacement. We identify the faucet type, match the internal part, and test under pressure so the fix holds.

Handle snapped off and water still running?

Find the shutoff valve under the sink or behind the access panel and turn it clockwise to close. If no fixture valve works, close the main shutoff where the water service enters the house, often near the meter or in the basement. Keep clear of wet outlets and do not touch the electrical panel if there is standing water.

Then call (732) 466-1098. A stuck-open faucet can flood a floor quickly, and a broken shutoff valve is just as urgent. Photograph any damage for your insurer or landlord, and tell us the faucet type, if you know it.

Showers and tubs: diverters and valves

Shower faucets hide their trouble inside the wall, so symptoms are the best clue.

Water from the tub spout while the shower runs

A worn diverter lets water take the wrong path. Spout diverters are a simple fix, while wall diverters need a cartridge.

Shower drips after shutoff

A worn cartridge or seat is likely. Dripping into the wall is a reason to act soon.

Temperature swings

A pressure-balancing cartridge may be clogged or worn. Other fixtures on the line can cause the same thing.

Handle stiff or loose

Mineral scale can grip the stem. Loose handles often have a stripped screw or worn cartridge.

Aerators, scale and the 72 to 140 ppm range

New Jersey American Water reports naturally occurring hardness of 72 to 140 ppm in its Coastal North system, about 4 to 8 grains per gallon. Your street may sit anywhere in that moderately hard to hard range. Minerals collect in aerators, on cartridges and around spouts.

The practical result is a reason to check the simplest part first. Unscrew the aerator, rinse out the grit, and soak it in vinegar if it is crusty. If the pressure returns, you saved a service call. If not, call us. We also clear scale from cartridges when we can, and replace them when we cannot.

Before we arrive

A few quick steps keep the damage small.

  • Close the fixture shutoff valve or, if it fails, the main shutoff.
  • Open the faucet to drain pressure from the line.
  • Clear the cabinet under the sink so we can reach the valves.
  • Place a towel and bucket under any drip, and keep children clear.
  • Photograph the damage, and note the faucet brand if it is printed anywhere.
  • Keep any parts that came off, such as handles or caps.

When the shutoff valve itself fails

Old angle stops and multi-turn valves seize or leak when turned, which is why they get ignored for years. If a valve will not close, or drips at the stem, the repair starts one step upstream.

We replace failed fixture valves with quarter-turn shutoffs and new supply lines. If the valve is on a section of pipe that cannot be isolated, we shut the main and handle the job from there. Our toilet repair work uses the same kind of valve at the wall.

Outdoor spigots: freezing and vacuum breakers

An outdoor spigot that leaks inside the wall when turned on may have a cracked body from freezing. A drip from the top of the handle can be a worn packing nut, and water coming out of a small vent on the side is usually a vacuum breaker doing its job.

Disconnect hoses before cold weather and close the interior shutoff to the line if one exists. A frozen spigot can burst without showing, so look for a wet wall when you turn it on in spring. About 29.5% of housing units in Belmar are vacant, many of them seasonal, so closing and draining outdoor lines before you leave for the winter pays off there and in Neptune City.

Repair or replace the whole faucet?

Repair makes sense when the body is sound and parts are available. Replace when the finish is peeling, the body is cracked, or the repair cost nears a new faucet. A new fixture also lets you upgrade to a lower-flow aerator or a better design. We tell you which way is cheaper before work starts.
