Drain cleaning is the removal of a blockage from the pipes that carry wastewater out of your home, whether the clog sits in one fixture trap or in the main line. If a sink, tub or floor drain in your Neptune home is backing up right now, our 24/7 emergency line is open around the clock. Stop running water, keep everyone out of any pooled water near outlets, and call (732) 466-1098.

We start by working out where the blockage sits, because a clogged tub and a clogged main line need different tools. Then we clear it and run water to confirm the line flows. You get a clear price before work starts.

Water draining slowly or backing up?

A slow drain is a partial blockage. Grease, hair, soap film or scale has narrowed the pipe, so water still passes but slowly. A drain that backs up has lost most of its opening, and the water returns out of the lowest fixture.

Plumbers call both problems a clogged or restricted drain line, and the fix is drain cleaning. The useful question is how many fixtures are affected. One slow sink points to its own branch. Several slow fixtures, or a tub that gurgles when the washer drains, point to the main line.

Match the symptom to the drain

Where the trouble shows up tells us where to start.

Kitchen sink

Slow draining with a greasy smell usually means cooled fat coating the trap and the run behind it.

Bathroom sink

Hair and toothpaste residue bind together just below the stopper.

Tub or shower

Standing water at your ankles points to a hair mat. If the toilet bubbles too, the clog is deeper.

Basement floor drain

Water rising here during laundry or a storm means a main line problem, not a local clog.

Whole-house slowdown

Gurgling and slow flushing together mean the main line has narrowed, often from roots or scale.

What clogs drains in the Neptune area

Neptune is mostly single-family housing, with newer townhomes, condos and multifamily buildings added over the years. Older houses, including the Victorian wood-frame stock in Ocean Grove built roughly between the 1870s and the 1920s, may still carry older drain materials. We cannot tell you the pipe type from the address, so we check with a camera when clogs keep coming back.

Grease and wipes cause the most avoidable trouble. The Township of Ocean Sewerage Authority publicly warns that flushed wipes and grease damage collection systems in neighboring Ocean Township. The lesson holds on your side of Route 66 as well: wipes labeled flushable do not break down like toilet paper, so bin them.

Municipal water here runs from 72 to 140 ppm hardness, so your street may sit anywhere in that moderately hard to hard range. Mineral scale builds slowly inside drains and gives grease and hair something to catch on.

How a drain cleaning visit runs

Every blockage differs, but this is the sequence on a typical call.

  1. Locate the clog

    We test which fixtures drain and which do not, then pinpoint whether the blockage sits in a trap, a branch or the main.

  2. Open access

    We use the nearest cleanout, or pull the trap or toilet if no cleanout exists, so the cable runs straight.

  3. Clear it

    We pick the auger, cutting head or jet nozzle that fits the material and pipe size.

  4. Run water and explain

    We flow water for several minutes, check the joints we opened, then tell you what caused the clog and what to change.

Before you call, and what to skip

A few steps help, and a few make things worse.

  • Stop running water in every fixture once something backs up.
  • Use a plain plunger on a sink or tub, but never a plunger after chemical drain cleaner has gone in.
  • Skip caustic drain openers, which sit in a standing clog and can burn skin or damage older pipe.
  • Note which fixtures are affected and photograph any water on the floor.
  • Clear the area around the floor drain or cleanout so we can reach it.

When cleaning is not enough

If the same drain stops up again within weeks, the problem is rarely the clog itself. It may be a sag in the pipe that holds water, roots entering at a joint, or a cracked section. We recommend a sewer camera inspection before paying for a third cleaning.

Replacing sections of pipe is repair work, not cleaning, and permit rules vary, so ask the Neptune Township construction office. For clogs deeper in the main, see sewer line cleaning.
