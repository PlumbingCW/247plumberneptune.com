Ocean Township, NJ plumbing at a glance

Coverage

We cover Ocean Township in Monmouth County, not Ocean County. That means Oakhurst in ZIP 07755 and Wanamassa and Wayside in ZIP 07712.

Dispatch

Emergency dispatch is available around the clock, dispatched from our base at 3297 Route 66 in Neptune. Call (732) 466-1098.

Housing

The township covers 10.99 square miles and counted 11,541 housing units in the 2010 Census. That is a wide spread of lot sizes and pipe ages.

Water and sewer

Drinking water is on the New Jersey American Water Coastal North list. Wastewater treatment is handled by the Township of Ocean Sewerage Authority.

Most plumbing questions in Ocean Township come down to one pipe: the one that carries waste out of the house and eventually to a treatment plant on Roosevelt Avenue in Oakhurst. When that pipe slows, the symptoms show up in tubs, floor drains and toilets long before anyone sees a puddle.

This page starts with water and sewer, because both shape what we recommend. We cover the township from Oakhurst through Wanamassa and Wayside, and we dispatch from Neptune. We have no office here.

Who treats your wastewater, and why it matters to your drain

The Township of Ocean Sewerage Authority, known as TOSA, provides wastewater treatment for Ocean Township, Deal, Allenhurst, Interlaken and Loch Arbour. It serves about 30,000 residents from a plant and offices on Roosevelt Avenue in Oakhurst.

For a homeowner the practical point is simple. What leaves your house shares pipes with thousands of others. A blockage that starts in your lateral can back up into your home first, so ask the township or the authority where your private lateral ends and the public side begins.

Wipes and grease: the two things TOSA publicly warns about

TOSA publicly warns that flushed wipes and grease cause costly problems in collection systems. We see the same two culprits on the household side. Grease cools and hardens in the horizontal pipe past the kitchen sink, and wipes snag on any rough spot and build a mat.

Our drain cleaning in Ocean Township uses a machine with a cutting head sized to the pipe, followed by a camera when the problem repeats. Wipes can often be pulled out. Grease scale may need a jet nozzle to strip the wall.

When the lateral needs repair, not another cleaning

If the camera finds a collapsed section, a major offset or roots through a broken joint, cleaning only buys time. Sewer line repair options include spot repair, lining the pipe from inside, pipe bursting and open-trench replacement. Each trades cost against disruption to your lawn and driveway.

Permit rules vary, so ask the Ocean Township construction office what your repair needs. For buyers, a sewer inspection before closing shows the lateral's condition on video.

Oakhurst, Wanamassa and Wayside: which ZIP is which

Oakhurst uses ZIP 07755, and we dispatch there for the most common calls, including clogged drains and sewer laterals. Wanamassa and Wayside use ZIP 07712, a code Ocean Township shares with Asbury Park.

Other localities include Deal Park, Green Grove, West Deal, Oakhurst Manor and Shadow Lawn Manor. Give us the neighborhood name along with the street when you call, since a shared ZIP can describe two very different parts of town.

Neighborhood lots

Mixed lot sizes mean mixed run lengths. A long lateral across a deep yard costs more to repair than a short one.

Before we arrive: a drain backup in an Ocean Township home

Sewage-contaminated water is a household safety issue, so go slowly.

Shut off the water

Close the main valve and stop using sinks, tubs, the dishwasher and the washer. Every gallon you run can end up on the floor.

Avoid wet electrical areas

Stay out of a flooded basement if the water reaches outlets, a panel or appliances. Do not walk into standing water near them.

Clear the area

Move belongings off the floor near the floor drain and cleanout. Keep children and pets away from the backed-up water.

What we do not cover on this page

A leaking roof or cracked foundation is not a plumbing problem. Neither is an oil burner fault or a tripped panel. For public sewer main questions, ask the authority or the township, not us.
