Ocean Grove is the square mile of Neptune Township that began in 1869, when the Ocean Grove Camp Meeting Association marked out about 300 acres between what is now Wesley Lake and Fletcher Lake. Plumbing calls here have a few features of their own, including a separate sewerage authority, seasonal tent structures and a flood history that shapes what a basement can safely hold.

This page covers the questions Ocean Grove property owners ask most: who owns the sewer main, how to bring a seasonal structure back to life in spring, and what to check after water gets in. Local details come from the township's neighborhood page, the National Park Service record for the historic district and the state's 2014 recovery report.

Whose pipe is blocked: your lateral or the main?

Ocean Grove has its own sewerage authority, and the township says it keeps a separate Sewer Maintenance Department for the rest of Neptune. That split matters when a floor drain backs up, because the owner of the main depends on where the street sits. Ask the sewerage authority or the township which one serves your block before you assume a clog is yours.

Your side of the line, the lateral from the house to the main, is yours to keep clear. A camera run through the cleanout shows whether the blockage is roots, grease or a collapsed joint. Start with sewer line inspection, then decide between sewer line cleaning and repair based on what the picture shows.

Bringing a seasonal tent or cottage back for the summer

The township describes the tents around the Great Auditorium as a wooden rear structure holding the bathroom and kitchen, with a canvas front section over a wood floor. The tenting season runs from Memorial Day to Labor Day. Any plumbing that sits idle for months deserves a check before guests arrive.

Open the supply slowly

Turn the water on at the main and walk every fixture. Watch the meter dial with all taps closed to catch a slow leak.

Look under every sink

Idle supply lines and trap fittings can weep after a winter. A dry paper towel under each joint shows drips quickly.

Check the water heater

Look for rust, damp insulation or a puddle before you switch it on. A tank that leaks at the seam needs replacing, not repair.

Close it down in fall

Drain or protect lines that could freeze. Burst pipe repair after a freeze costs far more than a shutdown visit.

Historic district: ask before changing the outside of the house

The Ocean Grove Camp Meeting Association District was listed on the New Jersey Register in 1975 and the National Register in 1976. A listing alone does not tell you what a homeowner may change, and we do not know which approvals apply to your address. Before work that alters the exterior, such as a new water heater vent, ask the Ocean Grove Community Association and the Neptune Township construction office.

Wesley Lake, Fletcher Lake and what Sandy showed

The state's recovery report named Ocean Grove among the most damaged and most flood-susceptible neighborhoods after Superstorm Sandy in 2012, with six structures judged unsafe. It also found that Wesley and Fletcher Lakes silted in and needed de-silting and storm drain cleaning. The practical lesson is to know your flood zone and to keep storm drains near your property clear of leaves and debris.

If water has entered a lower level, stay away from wet electrical areas and do not touch the panel while standing in water. Have a water heater or boiler that sat in floodwater inspected before you restart it. The township requires a Floodplain Development Permit before construction begins, so ask the construction office before you raise or rebuild anything.

What to tell us when you call from Ocean Grove

A few details make the first conversation shorter and more useful.

  • The street and nearest cross street.
  • Whether it is a house, cottage, apartment or tent structure.
  • Whether the water is shut off and where the main valve is.
  • What you see, hear or smell and when it began.
  • Whether anyone rents the property and who to notify.
  • Photos or a short video if you can take them safely.
