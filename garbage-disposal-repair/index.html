Disposal humming but not spinning?

A humming disposal that will not turn is almost always jammed. The motor is getting power, but a spoon, bone, fruit pit or hardened scrap has locked the grinding plate. Running it longer only burns out the motor.

Technically this is a seized impeller, and the usual fix is to turn the plate backward by hand. Silence with no hum is a different problem, often a tripped overload button or a failed switch or cord. Three steps below cover the jam safely.

Clearing a jam without hurting yourself

Never put your hand into a disposal, even when it is off. Use these steps instead.

  1. Cut power

    Switch off the wall switch and unplug the unit, or turn off its breaker if it is hardwired.

  2. Look with a flashlight

    If you see the object, lift it out with tongs or pliers.

  3. Turn the plate

    Fit a hex key into the socket in the center of the underside and work it back and forth until it spins freely.

  4. Press reset

    Wait a few minutes, then press the red reset button on the bottom of the unit.

  5. Test with water

    Restore power, run cold water and flip the switch. If it hums again, stop and call us.

Water pouring from under the sink right now?

Shut the cold supply valves under the sink if you can reach them, then call (732) 466-1098. Turn the disposal off at the switch and unplug it. A leak from a disposal sits right above the cord and cabinet wiring.

If water has spread across the floor toward outlets or appliances, stay clear and do not touch the panel while standing in water. Photograph the damage and move items out of the cabinet. If only a few drops fall each minute, place a bucket and book a same-day appointment when available.

Double sinks, shared traps and the dishwasher line

On a double sink, the disposal usually discharges into a shared trap and drain with the second bowl. When that drain clogs, water from one side rises in the other. The cause is the pipe beyond the disposal, so a new unit will not fix it, though drain cleaning will.

The dishwasher hose also connects to the disposal inlet. When a new disposal is installed, the inlet has a knockout plug that must be removed or the dishwasher will not drain. Many drain-back complaints trace to a plug left in, or a hose with no high loop.

What never belongs in a disposal

Most repairs start with something that should not have gone down.

  • Grease, fat and oil, which cool and coat the pipe beyond the unit.
  • Coffee grounds in quantity, which pack into sludge.
  • Bones, fruit pits and corn husks.
  • Pasta, rice and potato peels, which swell into paste.
  • Fibrous scraps such as celery, onion skins and artichoke.
  • Eggshell fragments in large quantity and any non-food item.
  • Chemical drain openers, which attack seals and the unit itself.

Kitchens here: seasonal homes, rentals and condos

Neptune includes single-family houses, townhomes, condos and multifamily buildings, and in many of them the kitchen drain connects to a shared stack. A disposal-related backup in a multifamily building can show up in a neighbor's sink, so report it to your property manager at once. Take photos and note which units are affected.

A disposal left unused for months can rust, seize and leave a dry trap that lets sewer odor into the kitchen, so run water and test it on arrival.

Repair or replace the disposal?

A jam, a reset or a loose flange is worth repairing. A cracked housing, a leak at the bottom, a motor that smells burned or a unit that has failed more than once is not. Replacement goes fastest when the mounting ring matches, and permit rules vary, so ask the Neptune Township construction office if you are changing fixtures.
