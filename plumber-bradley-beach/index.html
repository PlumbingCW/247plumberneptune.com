Closing the house for the season: a shutoff plan that works

A vacant house loses water silently, because nobody sees the drip. The simplest protection is to shut the main valve before you leave, then open the lowest faucet and the highest to let the lines drain. Tell a neighbor or manager that the water is off.

If heat stays on, a pipe can still fail, but the damage is limited when the main is closed. Water heaters should be turned off or set to vacation mode first, since a tank with no water in it can overheat.

A burst pipe in a closed house: what to do first

Shut the main valve. If you cannot reach it, call the 24/7 line at (732) 466-1098 and tell us. Do not use an open flame to thaw anything. Burst pipe repair means cutting out the split section and replacing it with a coupling or new pipe, then restoring water slowly while we watch for further leaks.

Photograph the damage and the shutoff before you clean. Insurers and landlords will ask. If water reached outlets or the panel, stay out and call an electrician.

Slow leaks that only show up in summer

Not every leak is dramatic. A weekly rental with guests running showers and washers can reveal a hidden drip behind a wall. If the water bill looks high, close every tap and check whether the meter dial still moves.

When the meter moves and nothing is visibly wet, leak detection with a pipe microphone or thermal camera finds the source. Billing differs by town, so ask your billing authority how your water is metered.

Storm season and the water close to Fletcher Lake

Bradley Beach's boardwalk came through Superstorm Sandy intact, but the borough's Sandy damage to beach areas and homes exceeded $3 million. Fletcher Lake is co-managed with Neptune Township through a commission, and low basements near water see seepage after hard rain.

A sump pump with a battery backup is the standard fix for a basement that takes water. It is only useful if the discharge line is clear and the pump is tested before the storm. Power can fail during the very storm you need it for.

What a returning owner should check

Walk the house before you turn everything on.

Water heater

Look for rust, moisture or scorch marks before you restore power or gas.

Supply valves

Check under each sink and behind each toilet for crusted or damp fittings.

Floor drains and traps

Pour water into rarely used drains so the trap seals again.

Outdoor spigots

Open them slowly. A split inside the wall shows up as a spray indoors.

Before we arrive: a Bradley Beach house with a water emergency

With so many homes empty part of the year, a leak can run unseen, so shutting the water off fast matters.

Shut off the water

Close the main valve, then open a low faucet to drain the line. Note where the shutoff is so you can tell us.

Avoid wet electrical areas

Do not step into standing water near outlets or a panel. Switch off breakers only from a dry spot.

Clear the area

Move belongings above the waterline and make sure we can get in. Absent owners should arrange a key holder.
