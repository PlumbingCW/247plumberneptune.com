Water heater replacement is the full swap of a failed or failing heater: shutting off and draining the old unit, hauling it out, setting the new one, and reconnecting water, fuel, venting and safety devices. Most households learn they need one on the worst morning, with a cold shower or a wet utility room. Our licensed plumbers answer the 24/7 emergency line around the clock, and same-day appointments are offered when available.

A good replacement is more than a like-for-like swap. The old install may have skipped an expansion tank, used a short vent run, or left no drain pan. We check those items against the National Standard Plumbing Code, then give you a clear price before work starts.

Heater dead or flooding the utility room right now?

Close the cold water valve above the heater first. If water is pouring out, switch the heater off at its breaker only when the panel is dry and you are not standing in water. Otherwise stay out and call us.

If you smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 before you call a plumber. A cold shower can wait, a gas leak cannot. Once the building is cleared, call (732) 466-1098 and tell us the fuel type, tank size and where the leak starts.

Replace now or repair once more

A leak from the tank shell ends the debate, since steel that has rusted through cannot be patched. Water at a fitting, valve or element flange is a different story and often a repair, which is why we look before quoting.

Age, repeat failures and a rusty bottom all push toward replacement. If you are leaning the other way, our page on water heater repair explains which parts are worth saving.

Sizing: 40, 50, 75 and the first-hour rating

Tank size is only half the story. The first-hour rating tells you how much hot water the unit delivers in the first sixty minutes when full and hot, and it is printed on the yellow label.

40 gallons

Fits one or two people with modest demand. Two showers back to back may drain it.

50 gallons

The common choice for a family home. A 50 gallon water heater covers most mixed use without needing a larger closet.

75 gallons

Suits large households or soaking tubs. It needs more floor space and a bigger fuel load.

Match the first-hour rating

Add up your busiest morning's fixtures. If the rating falls short, size up or consider a second unit.

What a proper swap includes beyond the tank

These items are where cheap replacements cut corners.

  • Gas line and shutoff check, including a leak test at the new connection.
  • Venting inspected for slope, joints and clearances, and corrected if it fails.
  • Expansion tank sized and installed on closed systems, with air pressure set.
  • Drain pan with a pipe to a safe drain or outdoor termination.
  • Temperature and pressure relief valve with a full-size discharge pipe.
  • New water connectors and shutoff valve, not reused fatigued ones.
  • Old unit removed and hauled away.

Permits and inspection in Neptune Township

Permit rules vary, so ask the Neptune Township construction office what your replacement needs. The office has a Plumbing Subcode Official and an online permit portal. The township's 2018 fee schedule lists $27 per fixture or device, including water heaters up to 99 gallons, and $65 for a heater of 100 gallons and up. Confirm current fees with the office.

Inspections must be requested at least 24 hours ahead, and the township says it performs them within three business days of the requested time. Plan for that gap, because an unpermitted install can create trouble at resale. For planning a brand-new location instead, see water heater installation.

How a replacement day runs

Each step below ties to the heater, not to a generic job list.

  1. Isolate

    We close water, power or gas, and open a hot tap so the tank can drain.

  2. Drain and disconnect

    A hose carries the water to a drain, then lines, vent and relief piping come off.

  3. Set and level

    The new unit goes on the pan, and any failed venting or supply piping gets corrected.

  4. Fill and purge

    The tank fills with a hot tap open to push out air, then we check every joint for drips.

  5. Fire and test

    We light or energize the unit, check flue draw or element power, and set the temperature in the 120 to 140 degree F range.

Warranty, explained in general terms

Tank heaters usually carry a manufacturer warranty on the tank and a shorter one on parts, and labor is often a separate matter. Read the actual terms on the unit you choose, since coverage varies by brand and model. Warranties generally expect proper installation, and some ask for regular maintenance, so keep your paperwork.

What 72 to 140 ppm water means for the new tank

New Jersey American Water reports hardness of 72 to 140 ppm in its Coastal North system, about 4 to 8 grains per gallon, and your street may sit anywhere in that range. Heated hard water drops scale inside a tank.

The payoff: ask your installer about flushing, anode rod condition and a drain valve you can reach. Those small choices stretch the life of the next heater, which matters just as much in Neptune City.
