Local to Neptune

What Neptune's water, soil and housing mean for your plumbing

Neptune Township sits between the Shark River and Deal Lake, and that water shapes the calls we plan for. After Superstorm Sandy in 2012, the state's recovery report named Shark River Hills, Ocean Grove and North Island as the most flood-susceptible neighborhoods. If your home is in one of those areas, a sump pump with a battery backup and a main shutoff you can find in the dark matter more than they would inland.

Tap water here comes from New Jersey American Water's Coastal North system, which reports hardness between 72 and 140 ppm as calcium carbonate. That is the moderately hard to hard range, so scale builds up in water heaters and faucet aerators over time. Your street may sit anywhere in that range, so flushing a tank water heater on a regular schedule is a sensible habit.

The Ocean Grove Camp Meeting Association District, a National Register of Historic Places district, covers buildings dating from about 1850 to 1924. Houses that old can still have original piping behind the plaster. That is why we diagnose before we quote a repair, and why a leak detection visit often comes before any wall is opened.

Many plumbing jobs here need a township permit and an inspection. Permit rules vary, so ask the Neptune Township construction office before work begins. We tell you when a job is likely to need one, which matters most for water heater replacement and sewer work.

Sources referenced on this page: New Jersey American Water, Coastal North annual water quality report. NJ Department of Community Affairs, Strategic Recovery Planning Report on Neptune Township (2014). NJ Division of Consumer Affairs, State Board of Examiners of Master Plumbers. National Park Service, National Register of Historic Places record for the Ocean Grove Camp Meeting Association District.
