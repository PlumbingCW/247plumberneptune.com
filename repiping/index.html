Repiping means replacing the water supply pipes that run through a house, either all of them or a failing section, with new copper or PEX. It is the answer when a house keeps springing leaks in different places, delivers rusty or weak water, or carries a pipe material known to fail. Drains and sewer lines are a separate system. If you have an active leak right now, close the main valve and call (732) 466-1098.

A repipe is a big job, so it should start with proof. We find where the leaks are, check the pipe wall, and test pressure before recommending anything. Sometimes a repair is the honest answer, and we will say so.

Signs the supply pipes are wearing out

One symptom does not mean a repipe. A pattern across the house does.

Repeated pinhole leaks

A second or third leak in a different spot suggests the whole run is thinning from the inside.

Brown or orange water

Rust flakes after a quiet night often come from galvanized steel corroding from within.

Weak flow at every fixture

Mineral scale and rust narrow old pipes until a shower cannot run while a dishwasher fills.

Green crust at fittings

Blue-green stains on copper joints show a slow weep that will not stop on its own.

Wall stains and a metered drip

A meter that turns with taps off and damp patches on walls point to concealed damage. See leak detection to confirm.

Galvanized, copper and polybutylene: what each fails from

Older supply materials die in different ways, which changes how urgent a repipe is.

Galvanized steelRust builds inside until the bore shrinksLow pressure, brown waterRepipe is usually the cure.
Copper (thin wall)Pinholes from pittingSmall leaks and green spotsRepipe after repeat failures.

CPVC (aged)Brittle cracks at fittingsSplits after temperature swingsReplace failing runs.

Polybutylene is the gray or blue flexible plastic pipe used in some homes built from the 1970s into the mid-1990s. If you see it at the water heater or main, ask us for an assessment.

Neptune's water and what it means for copper

New Jersey American Water reports hardness of 72 to 140 ppm as calcium carbonate in its Coastal North system, and the water is treated with ortho-phosphate for corrosion control. That film helps protect pipe walls, but it cannot restore a pipe that is already thin. Your decision still comes down to the age and condition we find.

Hardness leaves scale inside old steel and at fittings, so it speeds the loss of flow in galvanized lines. If the buried line to the house is also old, water service line repair may belong in the same plan.

Victorian walls in Ocean Grove and similar older homes

Ocean Grove's Camp Meeting Association District is a National Register of Historic Places district, with Queen Anne, Stick and Craftsman homes from the periods 1850 to 1924. Wood-frame walls in these homes can hold decades of past work, with plaster, tight stud bays and runs that were moved over time. Access, not pipe cost, is often the hard part.

Because historic-district review may apply, check any historic-district review that applies before opening finished walls or changing visible trim. We plan chases through closets, basements and attics first. Homes of the same era in Asbury Park present similar access puzzles.

PEX or copper: the honest trade-offs

Both are accepted for supply piping under the National Standard Plumbing Code. The right pick depends on access, budget and how you feel about each material.

Freeze toleranceExpands more before splittingSplits sooner under ice.

Many houses end up with both, such as PEX in the walls and copper at exposed or high-heat spots.

Whole house or one section at a time?

A phased repipe replaces one floor, one bathroom group or one branch at a time. It spreads out cost and disruption, and it can follow a kitchen or bath remodel. The risk is that untouched old lines keep failing while you wait.

A full repipe is faster overall, uses one inspection plan and ends the pinhole chase. If the leak history shows several failures across floors, we usually advise doing it once. If the failure is one stub and everything else tests sound, a targeted fix is better. We will not push the bigger job.

How a repipe unfolds in an occupied house

Planning the order of rooms matters more than the pipe itself.

  1. Survey and map

    We trace each branch, mark fixtures, and decide where small access openings will go.

  2. Protect the house

    Floors, furniture and fixtures are covered before any opening is cut.

  3. Run the new lines

    New manifold or trunk lines are pulled through walls and framing, and old pipe is capped or removed.

  4. Pressure test and inspect

    The system is tested under pressure and then inspected before the walls are closed.

  5. Restore water, then patch

    Water comes back on room by room. Drywall and plaster are patched once the inspector signs off.

Walls, patching and who does the painting

Plumbers open walls and leave them patch-ready. Finish drywall, plaster, tile and paint are usually other trades. Ask about this when you compare quotes, and plan a painter or carpenter for after the inspection.

Permits and inspections for a repipe

Permit rules vary, so ask the Neptune Township construction office. The township's 2018 fee schedule lists $27 per plumbing fixture or device and a $95 minimum per subcode, and the office can confirm current fees. In Belmar, the Construction Department says unpermitted work can be fined $500 to $2,000.

For one and two family houses, the township requires rough plumbing inspection before walls are covered, finish plumbing for piping, trim and fixtures, and a final plumbing inspection. Request each at least 24 hours ahead. The township says it performs them within three business days of your requested time.

Questions to ask before you accept a repipe quote

Quotes can look alike and differ in scope. Put these questions to every bidder.

  • Which pipe material, and why that one for this house?
  • How many access openings, and who patches them?
  • Is the permit included, and who schedules the inspections?
  • Does the quote include the main shutoff and a pressure reducing valve check?
  • What happens to old pipe and any insulation or wrap that is removed?
  • How many days will the water be off, and what is the plan for those days?
  • Is the plumber licensed? Verify it on the state's License Verification System.

What drives the cost of a full-house repipe

Two houses of the same size can have very different repipe costs. These are the biggest drivers.

Number of fixturesMore baths, kitchens and laundry hookups mean more branches and more labor.

Insurance rarely pays for a planned repipe, though it may cover resulting water damage from a sudden burst. Ask your insurer before work begins.
